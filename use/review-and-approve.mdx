Seeing possible matches does not give DJ Support permission to change Spotify. Continue one explicit decision at a time.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the songs that need your choice">
    Compare your Rekordbox version with the possible Spotify match. If you allowed local listening, you can hear the exact Rekordbox version from your Mac.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record your match choices">
    Choose **Correct**, **Wrong — find another**, **Cannot verify**, or **Not my source**. DJ Support saves these as private, revisable match choices. Spotify has not changed yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Authorize the exact playlist change">
    Your local AI agent shows the playlist name, included and omitted songs, order, and privacy before asking permission. Authorizing this step creates or updates that private Spotify playlist.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Listen to the private playlist in Spotify">
    Check that it sounds right. Removing a song tells DJ Support not to reuse that proposed match. Creating the playlist alone does not make its matches reusable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose whether to remember the matches">
    DJ Support compares the playlist you reviewed with the version it created. Confirmed and corrected song matches can be remembered for later syncs; removed suggestions are remembered as rejected. Spotify does not change again during this step.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  Contributor documentation calls these stages **Preview**, **Qualification Draft**, **Provisional Playlist**, and **Approval**. The DJ-facing labels describe what you see and what changes.
</Info>

## When a proposal is wrong

- Use **Wrong — find another** to search for a different Spotify version.
- Choose the Spotify result yourself; a search result is only a suggestion.
- Use **Cannot verify** when you need to stop without inventing a verdict.
- Never put private playlist contents, file paths, or reports into a GitHub issue.
